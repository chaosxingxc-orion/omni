## 3. Comparator stability — the N0r arm earns its place, and reports two different answers

The noise-floor arm was added to institutionalize the P-SLU lesson. It repays that on the EM axis
and delivers an unregistered warning on the selection axis.

- **EM axis: perfectly stable.** 0/40 samples flipped EM=0 -> EM=1 on a byte-identical zero-supply
  re-fly. The A0 comparator does not spontaneously convert. S1's 16 conversions are therefore not
  re-fly noise — this is what makes the conversion numbers readable at all.
- **Selection axis: NOT stable.** All 40 N0r samples were tool-correct in A0 by construction, yet
  the re-fly lost **5/40 = 12.5%** of them (retention 0.8750) with the prompt held byte-identical.
  Tool selection on this carrier is reproducible only to about ±12pp at n=40.

That second figure is not a registered metric and changes no branch outcome, but it reframes one
clause: **SUPPLY-CONVERTS's "S1' tool-selection retention >= 95%" asks a supply arm to hold a
quantity whose own byte-identical replay drops 12.5%.** S1' lost 8/120 (6.7%) and S2 lost 9/120
(7.5%) — both *smaller* losses than the zero-supply re-fly itself recorded. The honest reading is
that neither supply arm demonstrably damaged tool selection; the 95% clause failed on a metric too
noisy to support it at this n. Recorded as a defect in the registered rule, not as a finding about
supply.

## 5. Why the S4 arm cannot answer the knowledge-vs-schema question

This is the most important structural finding of the read and it constrains how the branch verdict
may be cited.

The flight's registered supply shape is **roster replacement**, not roster augmentation: for
S1'/S2/S4 the arm's rows *replace* the `{tool_schemas}` system-prompt fill rather than being folded
in as an extra content part (`rails.probes.a2t_s2_flight`, "SUPPLY-SHAPE DECISION"). Only N0r keeps
A0's full 152-row registry. Therefore:

- **S1'** shows the core exactly the row(s) it itself picked in A0 — the correct tool, and nothing
  else.
- **S4** shows the core exactly ONE wrong tool's row, and **the correct tool is absent from the
  prompt entirely**.

Exact match requires the correct tool name. With the correct tool withheld, S4's `tool_accuracy` —
and hence its `exact_match` — can be non-zero only if the frozen core reproduces a tool name it is
no longer shown, from parametric memory, against an instruction that presents one alternative. It
never did (0/120). **S4's conversion rate of 0.0000 is therefore very largely structural, not
evidence that a wrong schema fails to convert.** The SCHEMA-ONLY branch, whose test is "S4 converts
about as well as S1'", was consequently close to unfirable by construction, and the SUPPLY-CONVERTS
clause "S4 conversion <= 5%" passed for a reason unrelated to what it was meant to detect.

The same applies to the S4-retention COPY-TRAP disjunct. Its registered rationale is explicit — "a
wrong row DRAGS selection away" — but a drag test requires the correct tool to remain available to
be dragged *from*. What actually happened is the opposite of dragging: given only a wrong tool, the
core **abstained in 111/120 cases with a well-formed empty call**, and emitted a non-empty wrong call
in at most 5. It did not take the bait; it declined to act. That is a genuinely reassuring integrity
result about the frozen core — it does not confabulate a call from an irrelevant schema — but it is
not the copy-trap the clause was written to catch, and this verdict does not count it as one.

**COPY-TRAP therefore rests on its induced-substitution disjuncts, which fired independently and on
the arms where the correct tool WAS present (S1' 34 vs 16; S2 34 vs 11).** Those are behavioural,
not structural. The verdict stands on them.

## 6. DOSE point (secondary, descriptive)

**MET: dilution costs conversion.** S1' 0.1333 (16/120) -> S2 0.0917 (11/120) when the roster grows
from the correct row(s) alone to 9 total entries — 5 fewer conversions, a **31% relative loss**, on
paired samples.

Two qualifications keep this from being over-read. First, 5 flips on 120 paired samples is a small
absolute difference, and no repeat-condition estimate of *supply-arm* variance exists (N0r bounds
re-fly noise for the zero-supply condition only). Second, the cost is concentrated rather than
uniform: tier1_direct 6->4 and tier7_multiturn 4->2 lose, tier5_needle 2->3 *gains*, and tiers 3/6/8
are 0 in both. Meanwhile the harm term does not improve with dilution at all — induced substitutions
are **34 in both arms**, identically. So dilution to K=9 buys nothing and costs roughly a third of
the conversions: on this evidence, a narrower roster dominates a diluted one, but the effect is a
descriptive n=120 observation, not an established dose-response curve.

## 8. What this settles for the L5 point on the supply-benefit curve

The Stage-1 read established a real +30.0pp target — 120 named samples where the frozen core selects
the right tool and still fails the call. The Stage-2 question was whether prompt-level supply of
tool-schema knowledge converts those failures, and whether any gain is knowledge or format. **The
answer is that supply converts a real but modest minority of them — 16/120, 13.3% — and that it
inflicts a larger amount of a specific, nameable harm while doing so: 34 injections of the supplied
row's literal default values into calls whose aligned gold argument differs.** That 2:1
harm-to-benefit ratio is the branch verdict, it is behavioural rather than structural, and it
survives dilution unchanged. The L5 point on the supply-benefit curve is therefore not merely lower
than hoped; it is *signed differently than the curve assumes*. Supply is not a monotonically
beneficial dose axis on this carrier — past the first row it adds a copy channel, and the copy
channel does not attenuate when the correct row is diluted among distractors. Any control plane that
treats "supply more schema" as a free improvement is buying 16 conversions and 34 corruptions.

**The knowledge-vs-schema question that motivated the SCHEMA-ONLY branch is NOT settled, and this
read must not be cited as settling it** (§5). The wrong-row arm withheld the correct tool, so its
0.0000 conversion is structural; the probe never ran the comparison it was designed around.
Provisionally the evidence leans away from the pessimistic "it's mostly format" reading — the
per-tier structure is knowledge-shaped, not format-shaped: conversion concentrates in tier1_direct
(26.1%) and tier2_parametric (17.6%) and is exactly 0 in tier3_multi_intent (0/29) and
tier8_intent_blending (0/16), which is a distinction about what the missing information *is*, not
about output shape. A pure formatting effect would not sort itself by compositional complexity that
cleanly. But that is an inference from the S1'/S2 tier profile, not a measured contrast, and
settling it needs a redesigned control that supplies a wrong row's schema **while leaving the
correct tool in the roster** — the same arm, augmenting instead of replacing. That is the single
highest-value next probe, and it is cheap: it reuses this flight's machinery with one changed line
in the supply shape.

Two further constraints on the +30pp target that this read hands forward. First, roughly half the
headroom is in tiers that supply demonstrably does not touch: tier3_multi_intent (29) and
tier8_intent_blending (16) converted 0/45 under both arms while absorbing the largest share of the
copy-trap. Single-row schema supply is not a compositional-failure remedy, and pooling those 45
samples into an average is what would make a supply arm look uniformly mildly effective when it is
in fact strongly effective on 40 samples and inert-to-harmful on 45. Second, the frozen core's
behaviour under a wrong-only roster — 111/120 well-formed abstentions rather than a forced wrong
call — says the deployment risk of narrowing a roster is *silence*, not *error*. For a control plane
that is the better failure mode, and it is worth knowing before OBS/ORG/SUPPLY/USE are tuned against
this carrier.

## 9. Limitations — read these with the numbers

- **Discovery split, n=120 (n=40 for N0r), descriptive only.** No confirmatory claim, no superiority
  claim, no generalization beyond this split, this quantization, this sampling policy, this prompt.
  One conversion is 0.83pp; a 5-flip DOSE difference is well inside what n=120 can support as
  suggestive only.
- **The S4 control is confounded by roster replacement** (§5). SCHEMA-ONLY was close to unfirable by
  construction and the S4-retention COPY-TRAP disjunct is structurally pre-loaded. Only the
  induced-substitution disjuncts carry the verdict.
- **Induced substitution is measured at bare argument-key granularity, not `(tool_name, key)`**
  (note 7). This was the deliberate, approved choice — a tool-name-scoped alignment would have made
  S4's count structurally zero and silently disabled COPY-TRAP's own named disjunct for the very arm
  it exists to catch. The cost is that a supplied default value which coincidentally matches a gold
  argument key belonging to a *different* tool in the same sample counts as a substitution. Exposure
  is bounded (evaluated per sample, typically 1–4 gold calls, against that sample's own 1–9 row
  roster, never the full 152-row registry) but non-zero, and it is the one place where the 34 could
  be an overcount. It cannot be an undercount from this cause.
- **The tier1_direct slot and induced columns are degenerate** (‡ above): 23/23 zero-argument golds
  make micro slot F1 0/0 and exclude every predicted argument from induced-substitution alignment.
  Argument over-generation on that tier — 23 of the 120 headroom samples — is invisible to both
  guardrail metrics. Any statement about "how much supply leaks" is computed on the other 97.
- **Slot P/R/F1 across arms compare different sample sets.** S1'/S2/S4 pool 120, N0r pools a
  different 40; the suite computes no S1'-restricted-to-N0r's-40 figure and this read did not derive
  one (that would be a second scoring pass). N0r's slot column is a within-arm descriptor, not a
  comparator for S1's.
- **No supply-arm noise floor exists.** N0r bounds re-fly variance for the *zero-supply* condition
  only (EM 0/40; selection 5/40). Nothing repeats S1', S2 or S4, so their run-to-run variance is
  unknown, and the DOSE difference has no variance estimate behind it.
- **Selection-axis instability is unregistered and material** (§3): 12.5% of byte-identical re-flies
  lost a previously-correct tool selection. Every retention number in this document — including the
  95% clause that sank SUPPLY-CONVERTS — sits on a metric noisier than the differences being read.
- **No scorer cross-check** (note 5): unlike the A0 read's 0/2400 recheck mismatches, nothing here
  independently validates the scoring, because the flight deliberately embedded no metrics.
- **Scorer semantics are interpretation, not ground truth.** `a2t-scorer-v1` reimplements the
  paper's metrics (order-insensitive multiset tool matching, case-sensitive names, canonicalized
  argument comparison, fail-closed parsing); those choices are load-bearing for every number above.
- **Branch rules were evaluated pooled** (note 10). The preregistration defines no tier-scoped
  variant, and the per-tier tables are a reporting obligation rather than a second evaluation — so
  the possibility that a tier-scoped rule set would have branched differently is untested by design.
- **The headroom set is a property of one A0 attempt.** A re-fly would not reproduce it exactly; the
  120 are a registered seed, not an established stable set.
