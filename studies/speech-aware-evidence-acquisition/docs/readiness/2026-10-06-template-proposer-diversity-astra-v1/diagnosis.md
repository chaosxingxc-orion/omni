# Source-bound diagnosis

This is a root-facing design analysis, not a new learner export or result verdict. The permitted history is Update2/3/4: T=3 complete populations, K=16 records each, 48 records total. Each complete system has M=4 nodes and was evaluated on the same reused N=20 inputs in its recorded source cohort. Update5 contributes only its already frozen proposal text/settings; none of its scientific outcomes are used here. Its two no-change positions, 08 and 15, remain spent.

## What is actually alike

The static audit reconstructs every complete Update5 system by applying its original changes to its exact active Update2 parent. All 16 complete systems differ in editable bytes. Solver, Reviewer and Planner each have 16 exact unique texts; Perception has 7. The 14 changed positions edit Perception in 3 positions, Solver in 10, Reviewer in 11 and Planner in 7. Only four positions change assembly: 03/07/11 move full-history/per-role review to latest-Solver/local-tools/per-role; 16 restores default query placement. Six assembly combinations remain represented.

It removes repeated reasoning as well as schemas, so it is not a pure boilerplate correction or semantic distance. Full definitions and machine-readable values are in static-audit.json. No model-based similarity judgment was invoked.

These figures do not support "all templates are copies." Necessary output contracts, task legality and tool/answer authority account for substantial repetition, while the remaining prose has low exact shingle overlap. Historical assembly combinations number 4, 7 and 5 for Update2, Update3 and Update4 respectively, versus 6 in the Update5 proposal; there is no monotonic assembly-collapse trend. Raw Solver/Reviewer/Planner similarity has not uniformly increased. A blanket demand to maximize textual distance would be poorly targeted and could damage interface compliance.

There are, however, recurring **decision-level themes** in the frozen proposal:

- Positions 01/09/16 emphasize constructing a complete object before serialization and reconstructing unusable output. They differ in accompanying review/assembly scope but share the primary representation hypothesis.
- Positions 02/06/11 emphasize a current anchor plus an operative criterion, distinguishing supported contradiction from a merely possible alternative, then accepting a supported decision. Their paragraphs differ, but the proposed downstream decision distinctions need clearer tests.
- Positions 04/12 both emphasize action/object/modifier relations for original-candidate operation choice and limited dependent revision. Position 05 additionally emphasizes argument attachment and legal applicability.
- Positions 07/13/14 emphasize different aspects of presence, role and extent while preserving unrelated supported content. These may be meaningful differences; their existence is not demonstrated by different nouns or role-text hashes.
- Position 03 is principally a history-scope test with compatible Reviewer/Planner wording, not a new semantic reasoning family. Treating that as an assembly experiment is reasonable.

These are qualitative readings of the complete emitted instructions, not demonstrated runtime equivalence or causal clustering. The current proposal already contains targeted hypotheses and partial-node edits. The improvement needed is to make contrasting decision conditions explicit and audit them against actual emitted text, rather than asserting diversity from unique wording.

## Why the present proposer can converge on similar advice

The old contract asks for "useful diversity" but never defines a non-cosmetic distinction, nearest-sibling contrast, mechanism coverage or failure signature. Its outcome priorities are sound. The author can comply by repeatedly paraphrasing evidence checks, preservation and precision across roles, especially when all branches inherit related parents and the same small feedback roster. This is a plausible generation bias, not a proved cause of measured performance.

Most branches can satisfy their rationales by adding "verify the current criterion" to existing guard-heavy role text. The actor is not required to say which evidence condition should make one branch accept while another requests a legal clarification, which repair dependencies change, or why an assembly choice is necessary. Shared instructions can also obscure each node's distinct decision responsibility. The revised prompt therefore adds a mechanism-first internal allocation, a nearest-sibling countercondition, explicit responsibility checks and an adverse signature. It does not force arbitrary verbosity or a shared scaffold.

## What the complete qualified history supports

Both Update3 and Update4 were rejected under the existing complete-population utility-sum rule; active search parents remain Update2 and the formal incumbent remains the seed. Update3's sum is approximately -59.594158 against -56.159254; Update4's is approximately -66.316477 against the same parent sum. These are registered utility sums, not pooled F1 or effect sizes. No Update4 candidate exceeds its own cohort seed's official SLU-F1, despite 100% reliable completion for all candidates. See the completed Update4 review and original retention receipts; do not retrofit a new acceptance rule.

A concrete aggregate example shows why more criticism is not a sufficient target. Update3 branch-07 has error detection 11/11 (Wilson95 0.741-1.000) and false alarms 9/9 (0.701-1.000), both with unknown=0. Alarm precision is 11/20 (0.342-0.742); this conditions on whether the draft is erroneous, not whether the criticism is semantically correct. Raw entity transitions across the 20 inputs record 0 repairs and 3 harms, unknown=0. The system completes reliably but its alarms do not distinguish correct from incorrect drafts.

The contrasting branch-02 has fewer alarms: in Update3 its detection is 7/13 (0.291-0.768), false alarms 2/7 (0.082-0.641), and entity repair given revision 0/5 (0-0.434), unknown=0 for each. Merely suppressing review is therefore no established solution either. Both examples are illustrative records from the complete 48-record capsule, not selected replacement learner data.

Missingness matters: Update4 branch-11's false-alarm observation is 6/7 known, eligible=8, unknown=1, Wilson95 0.487-0.974, with missingness bounds 0.750-0.875. Its raw entity/full transitions each show 1 repair and 3 harms across the 20 inputs, unknown=0. Postprocessing produces a different final entity transition count and must remain separate. Never flatten this to "86% false alarms on all correct drafts" or count postprocessing repair as model repair.

These small, reused-roster aggregates motivate testing criticism specificity, preserving correct drafts, and useful repair alongside better initial extraction. They do not identify which individual criticism is wrong, establish a causal failing node, prove knowledge sufficiency, or justify per-input access. Gold-intent card presence at 20/20 is a visibility measure, not complete entity-value support. Fixed decoding still has A/A differences: the completed Update3 remaining-b2 report shows seed/A/A F1 differing by 8.15 percentage points on the same 20 inputs. Do not optimize tiny point-estimate movements as established facts.

## Recommended change and tradeoff

Use the new prompt to search a deliberately contrasted set of bounded decision procedures: applicability, coverage, burden of criticism, discriminating counterfactual, relation-first interpretation, dependency-aware repair, current/history reconciliation, operation-only handoff, and representation construction. Keep existing data and knowledge fixed. Define the expected business/process signature and the cost or quality failure mode for each. Preserve inheritance and focused edits; they aid interpretation and need not look visually different everywhere.

Diversity can spend slots on mechanisms that are worse than a cautious local edit; focused designs can miss beneficial interactions, while joint designs make attribution harder. Strict criticism can miss real errors; broad coverage can cause false fills; full history can anchor, while local context can omit useful information. The matrix makes these tradeoffs testable. Its coverage counts are soft proposal guidance, not a guarantee of useful behavior or a new retention criterion. The strongest justified claim now is a clearer, compiler-compatible prospective search specification.
