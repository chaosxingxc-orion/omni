## A rule's value and applicability

A rule must specify an observable situation, a recommended evidence or reasoning
action, the discriminating or explanatory information the action should supply,
and a limitation or exception. It must not prescribe a sample's gold answer,
copy a transcript, or use an inaccessible test condition.

Examples of rule families, not dataset-derived findings:

- Candidate coverage: request a candidate refresh when the available evidence
  does not support the current candidate distinctions. The refresh must explain
  what new observation could resolve the ambiguity.
- Candidate selection: compare activation conditions and incompatible semantic
  cues across the surviving intent contracts; do not count repeated candidates
  as confidence.
- Slot grounding: use the candidate-specific field meaning and extraction
  constraints; prefer explicit acoustic support to an inferred missing value.
- Review and tools: request ASR or contract evidence for a named uncertainty;
  state what discrepancy would justify revision. Tool output remains evidence,
  and Solver retains final answer authority.
- Planner termination: distinguish a completed, supported answer from bounded
  exhaustion. Preserve a valid answer when an attempted extra step fails; record
  unavailable stages and unused observations.

The first candidate should preserve the existing graph, tool inventory, frozen
weights and knowledge releases. Concentrate the update in corrective guidance
and its role-aware consumption. A later change to candidate retrieval, graph
actions or task-knowledge construction requires its own named mechanism and
prospective configuration.

## Consolidation and selection requirements

The current proposal materializer is case-local: supporting_options resolves
only within one prepared case. The source record format can retain supporting
and contradicting reward-link references, but this does not itself implement
multi-case synthesis. Extend the existing public preparation, typed validation
and materialization abstractions; do not create an experiment-only optimizer.

Count distinct source utterances, not option count, as independent case support.
A proposed general rule needs at least two distinct TRAIN A cases to be labeled
multi-case; also report speaker and scenario coverage. This is descriptive
support, not proof of a rule's causal effectiveness. Preserve single-case
lessons as explicitly provisional evidence, outside the initial general-rule
runtime candidate.

Exact duplicate records may be merged deterministically. Semantically different
rules require model synthesis with explicit source labels; a lexical similarity
score cannot authorize combining incompatible conditions. Retain contradictions
rather than dropping them to make a candidate appear better supported.

The existing 32-record ceiling is a safety bound, not a selection strategy.
Choose a compact candidate based on complementary executable conditions,
nonredundant failure coverage and retained counterexamples. Do not take the first
32 records, choose by input order, or use Fold B performance for selection.
Root reviews the single candidate before freezing its execution configuration.

## Metrics and interpretation

Use the native full-task metric as the primary outcome, with natural-mixture
and equal-scenario summaries and paired speaker-cluster intervals. Report intent,
scenario/action and entity outcomes separately. Keep candidate coverage, accuracy
conditional on coverage, stage availability, tool usage, actual refreshes and
refresh information used by the delivered Solver as separate diagnostics.

A positive point estimate can support an observed improvement statement; a
reliable benefit requires its stated uncertainty and replication evidence.
An unhelpful TRAIN update can still identify a component-level failure direction.
It does not justify altering historical verdicts or tuning on Fold B.

Report actual source/proposal/consolidation/evaluation contacts and elapsed time.
Cost is descriptive for this owner objective; do not reject a quality-improving
system solely because its offline processing is slower. Keep one-shot exposure,
data isolation, role authority and zero paid research API boundaries intact.
