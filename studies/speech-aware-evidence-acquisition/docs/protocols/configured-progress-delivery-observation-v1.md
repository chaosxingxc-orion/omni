# Configured progress: native delivery custody

The observer is read-only. It counts the durable intent files in its directory enumeration as attempted contacts, including not-sent contacts; this is cumulative for the current attempt, not a polling delta or a replacement for historical attempt cost custody. Historical checkpoint and recovery counts retain their existing rules. FIT custody is reported separately.

Each committed intent is checked with `ContactJournal.inspect`, including request identity and hashes, live dispatch/not_sent boundaries, response bytes and retrospective reconciliation evidence. An observed response contributes validated metered usage and may be used by the learning progress bridge. A verified `not_sent` is counted separately and is not an observed response. A dispatch without a response, or an intent with no terminal evidence, is `inflight_or_unknown`; its spend is unknown. No classification authorizes retry or changes recorded historical costs. Known usage is only the sum of observed responses, not total attempt or historical spend.

Committed response, dispatch, not_sent and reconciliation files require a matching validated intent; conflicting or corrupted custody fails the job observation. If an atomic publisher creates an intent after the directory enumeration but its boundary appears in that enumeration, the observer validates the parent from disk without adding it to this snapshot's count. Responses and boundaries can become visible while inspection is underway, so this is not a transactional snapshot. A transient observation error must be investigated or observed again; it is not evidence of zero spend, nor permission to resend.

Atomic publication uses hidden regular files named `.<hash>.<kind>.json.<suffix>` for intent, response, FIT and delivery records. Their bytes may be incomplete and are reported as uncommitted, never used as custody; entries that vanish during enumeration are reported separately. Other filenames and non-regular entries fail observation. Reconciliation validation uses the production evidence paths and hashes, which must be accessible to the observer; unavailable evidence produces an observation error.
